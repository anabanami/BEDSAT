conditions constraining its dynamics. Unfortunately, the direct observation of subglacial landscapes is non-trivial since these are buried under kilometres of ice.
Currently, our capabilities to observe subglacial terrain rely mostly on indirect methods such as radar echo sounding (RES) and seismic sounding which return 1D profiles of the bedrock or 2D coverage from swath-radar interferometry, gravimetry and magnetics. Ice surface data from satellites can be used to inform basal topography models though extracting this information requires complex mathematical frameworks such as inverse modelling.

A crucial gap in subglacial mapping is that the relationship between bed topography and its corresponding morphometric landscape statistics has not been rigorously quantified at a continental scale. Additionally, landscape classification models are often limited to one or two morphometric quantities, which makes them vulnerable to survey artefacts such as local roughness measurement bias due to dataset migration or uneven coverage. Another issue with existing classification frameworks is that they frequently conflate measurably different bed properties under single discrete labels. This often results in averaged regimes which in turn fails to capture the true continuum of the subglacial landscape.

Here we introduce Observational Data Spectral Analysis (ODSA), a physically informed analysis framework for quantifying Antarctic subglacial landscape characteristics and classifying bed morphology into archetypes for ice sheet modelling. The ODSA framework explicitly addresses fundamental ambiguities inherent to historical radio-echo sounding (RES) data, specifically subglacial roughness anisotropy and spectral contamination across distinct geological domains. 
To prevent physically meaningless parameter averaging within RES transects, ODSA employs bedrock-gradient segmentation and surface-flow confidence weighting to ensure that extracted metrics represent homogeneous terrain. ODSA uses 50 km sliding windows to spatially analyse the RES transects, and each sliding window is described by a 13-element morphometric vector combining 5 classifying axes and 8 continuous descriptors. ODSA evaluates the morphometric vector against an 11-entry archetype catalogue, yielding an admissible set of landscapes for each analysed window, this prevents forced assignment of a single landscape label when the information available is ambiguous. 
Notably, since radar geometry alone cannot always distinguish between different landscapes, ODSA is built to flag classification ambiguities by tracking cases where distinct landscape archetypes produce similar spectral signatures (observational degeneracies). For instance, classifier axes are treated as Normal distributions with a variance based on regression scatter. If the uncertainty envelope of a classifier intersects with the allowed sets of multiple catalogue entries, ODSA admits all of them rather than forcing a deterministic fit. When a unit is labelled as degenerate, the pipeline explicitly names the reason for the overlap and identifies the external observable required to break the tie. Our framework provides the empirical foundation required to generate structurally realistic synthetic bed topographies for advanced inversion models.
