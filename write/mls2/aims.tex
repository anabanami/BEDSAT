\chapter{Aims}\label{aims}
My PhD project is a modelling approach that combines satellite observations of the ice surface, airborne radar surveys of the bed, and numerical ice flow modelling to improve our knowledge of the landscape beneath the Antarctic Ice Sheet, I call it \textbf{BedSAT: Antarctica}. Bed topography exerts a first-order control on ice flow and grounding-line stability, yet large parts of the Antarctic bed remain poorly constrained between radar flight lines. BedSAT addresses this by first characterising the observed bed through its morphometric and spectral statistics, using these to define a new physically-based classification of Antarctic subglacial landscapes. These statistics then drive the generation of synthetic bed topographies for simulations with the Ice-sheet and Sea-level System Model (ISSM)~\cite{Larour_2012}. Using machine learning trained on these physics-based simulations, I will learn how bed topography is transferred to the ice surface across different landscape types and flow regimes. Inverting this bed-to-surface transfer allows the surface expression observed in satellite data to be mapped back to bed topography, producing a new Antarctic bed topography product with improved accuracy, higher resolution, and landscape-aware uncertainties.

\section{Science Questions}
\begin{itemize}
\item{\textbf{SQ1}} To what extent can the subglacial landscape type be determined from along-track radar profiles alone, and which additional observables are needed where it cannot?
\item{\textbf{SQ2}} Which morphometric properties of the bed are expressed at the ice surface, and how do rheology and flow regime change it?
\item{\textbf{SQ3}} To what extent can bed topography be recovered from satellite observations of the ice surface by inverting a learned non-linear transfer function, and where does landscape type limit that recovery?
\end{itemize}

\section{Science Objectives}
\begin{itemize}
\item{(SQ1)} develop Observational Data Spectral Analysis (ODSA), a physically informed spectral framework for quantifying Antarctic subglacial landscape characteristics from radar observations. ODSA will be used to bound the landscape archetypes admissible for each segment of a radar track, and identify the observables needed to separate them, potentially providing a new statistical basis for representing bed topography in ice sheet modelling.
\item{(SQ2)} design a synthetic bed topography generator (Bedrock factory) that takes ODSA morphometric statistics for each landscape archetype as inputs and produces realistic bed realisations at ice sheet model resolution. Run ISSM simulations on ensembles of these beds to assess how landscape type and bed roughness influence ice flow and surface expression.
\item{(SQ3)} train a machine learning model on the physics-based ISSM simulations to learn how the bed expression is modulated through the ice onto the surface (bed-to-surface transfer function), and invert it using satellite observations of the ice surface to produce a new Antarctic bed topography product. The product will include uncertainty estimates informed by the landscape classification, identifying where the bed can be reliably recovered from the surface and where further radar surveys are most needed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Project Deliverables}
\begin{itemize}
    \item\textbf{Paper 1 — ODSA}: a window-level landscape classification framework for airborne radar echo sounding (RES) elevation profiles, with a catalogue of admissible archetypes and required externals.
    \item\textbf{Paper 2 — Bedrock factory}: an open-source synthetic bed generator for each landscape archetype. 
    \item\textbf{Paper 3 — BedSAT: Antarctica}: a new Antarctic bed topography product with landscape-dependent uncertainties.
\end{itemize}
